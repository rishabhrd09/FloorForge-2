DESIGN TRANSLATION
- Retain real 40 x 50 ft scale and room polygons. Spaciousness comes from aligned
  slab patterns, fewer competing materials, tall glazing and uncluttered routes.
- Use a physically scaled approximately 1.6 x 3.2 m porcelain slab appearance,
  fine low-contrast joints, subtle veining, and restrained satin/polished interior
  reflections. Outdoor walking finishes are matte, not wet mirror surfaces.
- Use ivory plaster, restrained limestone, teak doors/soffits and charcoal glazing
  frames. Show frame depth, tracks, jambs and handle hardware. Glass should be
  transmissive with reflections, not painted gray or opaque blue surfaces.
- Keep open terrace guards opaque/light, with coping and slight reveal detail.
  Interior gallery glazing can remain so the double-height space stays connected.
- Prefer aligned, simple column/fascia expression and modest supported projections.
  A structural engineer must size reinforcement, members and foundations for G+2;
  visual alignment is not structural verification.
- Garden terrace should have manageable planters, paved circulation, drainage
  and visible parapet enclosure. Avoid implying a heavy deep-soil lawn can be
  placed on any roof without checking saturated loads and waterproofing.

PRIMARY SOURCES CHECKED
Laminam — Sizes and Thickness:
https://www.laminam.com/usa/en/sizes-and-thickness/
Manufacturer lists 1620 x 3240 mm among its slab formats. These are full firing
sizes, not automatically final installed/rectified dimensions. It also lists
different thicknesses/applications; installation suitability remains project
specific. This supports a large-format rendering module, not a product order.

Schüco — Sliding systems:
https://www.schueco.com/schueco/architects/products/sliding-systems
Manufacturer describes large glass units, narrow visible frame widths, different
sliding/folding configurations and flush integration. This supports the appearance
of substantial modern glazing while retaining real frames/tracks and operation.

IIT Kanpur / NICEE — Earthquake Tip 25, Why are Load Paths Important in Buildings?
https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/EQTips/EQTip25.pdf
Primary engineering education source explains transfer through connected slabs,
beams, columns/walls and foundations, with continuous/direct gravity and lateral
load paths. The visual model should avoid floating columns and unexplained
interruptions. It does not establish project-specific structural adequacy.

Institution of Civil Engineers — Structural load paths:
https://knowledgehub.ice.org.uk/cpd/delivery-exc/structural-load-paths/
Explains floor-to-beam-to-column-to-foundation load transfer; removing supports
for an atrium redistributes load. Keep the approved double-height void, but
acknowledge its supporting structure requires whole-building analysis.

WBDG — Extensive Vegetative Roofs:
https://stg.wbdg.org/resources/extensive-vegetative-roofs
Guidance covers drainage, root/waterproofing protection, supporting structure and
additional wet growing-medium/occupancy loads. An accessible terrace garden is
not equivalent to a non-accessible extensive vegetative roof. Use this as a
coordination reference, not as local code compliance or a construction detail.

RENDER PIPELINE REVIEW
scripts/render_cycles.py is the stronger path: import the viewer presentation
GLB, preserve generated textures/foliage, use Cycles with Nishita sky, site sun,
AgX and camera metering. scripts/blender_scene.py reads native scene geometry but
uses only simple noise materials, missing the richer presentation textures.
New scoped adapter scripts/render_tirumala_5_cycles.py reuses render_cycles,
supports named scene cameras, recognizes ICU and T4 facade glass, preserves
physical UVs, and records its render settings. It never scales or replaces the
house geometry. Render results still require visual QA for exposure, camera
occlusion and any GLB export limitations.
